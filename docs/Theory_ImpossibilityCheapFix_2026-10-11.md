# Impossibility of a cheap-side fix for safety-cascade gate-collapse (theory note, 11 Oct 2026)

Precise statements of the two propositions that turn the gate-collapse result into a break+impossibility paper. Both are verified numerically in `tools/verify_theory.py` (T5, T6 green) alongside T1/T1c/T2/T4. Proof sketches are argument-level; the capacity leg of Prop 6 is an analogy to a known theorem, stated honestly as such.

## Setup (shared with Theorems_v4.md)
Cheap screen score s(x); escalate iff s(x) ≥ τ; expert blocks an escalated harmful x w.p. β(x); budget E_benign[1(escalate)] ≤ b. Harmful seed x0 has an attacker **feasible set** F(x0) = intent-preserving (compliance-preserving) transforms — the variants that still make the target comply with the harmful request (this is why the attack is useful, and why a capable expert still blocks many of them: β_A := inf_{x∈F} β(x) can be substantial in the char-level regime). β_A = expert recall at the attacker's chosen variant.

## Proposition 5 (certified-screen escalation PRESERVATION — the only sound certified fix)
Let the screen be certifiably robust with radius r in metric m: its escalate/no-escalate decision is invariant to any perturbation with m(x0, ·) ≤ r. Call x0 **certified-escalating at δ** if it is escalated clean (s(x0) ≥ τ) and its certified radius r(x0) ≥ δ; let ρ_δ = P_harmful(certified-escalating at δ), and α = the smoothing confidence slack.
- (i) **Recovery.** Any attacker restricted to ≤δ edits achieves worst-case cascade recall R_cert(δ) ≥ ρ_δ·β_A·(1−α), provided budget-feasibility **BF**: π_h·ρ_δ + (1−π_h)·f_b ≤ b (π_h = harmful base rate, f_b = benign false-escalation at τ).
- (ii) **Beats the floor.** If ρ_δ·(1−α) > b then R_cert(δ) > b·β_A strictly.
- (iii) **Limits.** Vacuous for attacks with footprint > δ (certificate void); probabilistic (w.p. 1−α).

**Correction that makes it sound (break-attempt, L1 discipline):** certification gives decision *stability from an escalated point*, NOT correctness — a stably-non-escalated harmful point is not helped. So the anchor "x0 escalated clean" is required. **Architecture constraint:** the gate must be a threshold on ONE smoothed screen's certified score; a *cascade of certified sub-classifiers picking the first label* is provably unsound (Perils of Cascading Robust Classifiers, 2206.00278 Thm 2.8 — which constituent certifies can flip across the ball). Prop 5 holds only under the single-certifier framing.
Verify: `verify_theory.py` T5 (within-δ recall = β_A > floor; beyond-δ no help).

## Proposition 6 (impossibility of a cheap-side certified fix)
A certified screen recovers worst-case recall on F(x0) (Prop 5-style) only if its m-ball of radius r **contains** F(x0): B_m(x0, r) ⊇ F(x0), equivalently coverage ρ = P_{x∈F}(m(x0,x) ≤ r) is non-trivial. Then one of three holds for any metric m a cheap screen could use (trichotomy):
- **(i) Surface metric** (edit distance / token-ℓ0 / embedding). The surface spread D_m(x0) = sup_{x∈F} m(x0,x) is **large** for intent-preserving char/Unicode obfuscation (MEASURED: edit distance ≈ 0.5·len; char-obfuscation is a *tokenization* attack that changes most tokens). Cheap smoothing certifies only small r ≪ D_m ⇒ ρ ≈ 0 ⇒ R_cert ≈ 0, reverting to the floor b·β_A. **No recovery.**
- **(ii) Compliance-tight metric.** A metric whose ball is small on F must decide compliance ≈ as hard as the target/expert ⇒ requires ≥ expert-level capacity ⇒ **not cheap**. Backbone is a PRINCIPLED ANALOGY (NOT a tight transfer — corrected after a full read): **"The Price of Robustness: Stable Classifiers Need Overparameterization," von Berg et al., ICLR 2026, arXiv 2603.02806** extends Bubeck–Sellke to discrete-OUTPUT classifiers (class-stability < (6K√c/ε)·√(p/(nd)), p = log-cardinality of a FINITE class), so substantial overparameterization is needed for stability. BUT it assumes CONTINUOUS inputs with c-isoperimetry and a finite hypothesis class (infinite/NN case open; isoperimetry unverified on real text); our attack is discrete/surface/tokenization. So cite it (and the continuous predecessor, Bubeck–Sellke NeurIPS 2021 / 2105.12806) as a strengthened analogy for "a cheap low-capacity screen cannot be robust where the high-capacity expert can," and explicitly state it does NOT transfer tightly to discrete surface-token perturbations. Do NOT quote p≈nd as our own bound. Concentration-of-measure limits (Fawzi 2018; Mahloujifar AAAI 2019) are adjacent but contested (later work: concentration not the sole cause, labels matter) — context only, do not rest the leg on them.
- **(iii) Canonicalization.** Map F(x0) to a canonical form then certify trivially — but a *complete* canonicalizer for intent-preserving transforms is impossible (Glukhov et al.; and decode-before-guard / DecipherGuard 2509.16870 is the partial, already-evaded instance).

**Conclusion.** No cheap (sub-expert-capacity) screen admits an (m, r) with B_m(x0,r) ⊇ F(x0) at non-trivial coverage. Certified recovery is confined to attackers whose *surface* footprint is genuinely small — which the measured char-level intent-preserving attacker is not (footprint ≈ 0.5·len). Hence the gate-collapse of Theorem 1 cannot be escaped by a cheap-side certified fix.
Verify: `verify_theory.py` T6 (surface leg): cheap radius 12 edits ⇒ coverage ρ ≈ 0.001, R_cert ≈ 0 ≤ floor; radius needed for ρ≥0.5 ≈ 38 edits (≫ achievable). Legs (ii)/(iii) are analytic.

**Falsifier (honest).** A cheap, tokenization-robust, certifiable metric with a non-trivial certified radius on homoglyph/fullwidth/spacing transforms would break Prop 6(i). None exists today: the strongest 2026 certs are surface/modality metrics — CSS (2602.01587) certifies discrete **token-substitution ℓ0**; Hybrid Randomized Smoothing (2605.12876, ICML'26) certifies joint **token + image** for multimodal filtering. Both read in full (methods) 2026-10-11; neither certifies char/Unicode obfuscation (char-level noise is explicitly a utility-breaker for CSS).

## Why this is the result (not just Cor 1.3 restated)
Cor 1.3 says no *delegation policy* beats the expert's β_A. Prop 6 says no *cheap screen-side defense mechanism* (the things a practitioner actually reaches for — certification, normalization, privacy, triggers) can bound the score-reachable intent-preserving attacker to escape that ceiling, each for a concrete, verified reason. That per-mechanism refutation — plus the measured convergence and the deployed target (Constitutional Classifiers++) — is the contribution.
